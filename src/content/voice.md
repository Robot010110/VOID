# The voice of VOID

Every word a visitor reads in VOID follows this guide: the story fragments on the twelve anchor
worlds, and the plain descriptions in the caption.

## Fragments

- **Short.** One to three sentences, never more than about 40 words.
- **Quiet, specific, human-scale.** One concrete image beats any amount of cosmic grandeur. A
  lamp at the end of a pier says more than a thousand years of history.
- **No exclamation marks.** No sci-fi jargon soup. No "ancient beings of pure energy". No "in the
  vastness of space".
- **Melancholy is welcome; despair is not.** The overall feeling is wonder. A fragment can end on
  loss, but the loss should leave something standing: a habit kept, a word kept, a light kept.
- **Never generated.** Generated poetry reads cheap. Procedural worlds get only a name and plain
  prose built from their actual properties.

Tone, from the brief:

> They built their cities on the night side, so the lights would face the stars.

> Every child here learns the same first word. It means "the sky is still there."

> The last broadcast from this world was a lullaby. It is still travelling.

> A cold ocean world. Its people left four thousand years ago, and the tide still comes in for
> them.

### Habits that keep fragments honest

- **Say what people did, not what they felt.** "They pointed" carries more than "they were in
  awe".
- **Ordinary objects and ordinary days.** A cradle, an almanac, a note on the table, low water.
  The scale of the universe is already on screen; the fragment supplies the human.
- **Plain numbers, said as people say them:** "a hundred years ago", "eleven generations". No
  dates, no units, no designations.
- **"They", not invented names.** A fragment never needs the people's name; the caption already
  gives it. Invented words in a fragment read as jargon.
- **True to the world on screen.** If a fragment mentions two moons, the world has two moons. If
  it mentions a ring, the ring is there. The visitor should be able to look up from the words and
  find what they describe.
- **Avoid the stock words:** ancient, eternal, infinite, vast, cosmos, whisper, echo, stardust,
  tapestry, journey, beings, energy, destiny.
- **Line breaks are part of the writing.** Break where the voice would pause, keep each line
  under about 45 characters, and let the last line land.
- **End on the image, not the moral.** "In case." "Was wave." Stop there.

## Descriptions

The caption beside the breadcrumb is not literature. It says what is there, in two or three plain
sentences:

- What the place is and what it looks like: "A temperate world of wide oceans and scattered
  islands."
- What is true of it: its day, its moons, its people and how long they have lived there.
- No metaphors, no mood, no second person. Spelling is British: colour, grey, towards.
